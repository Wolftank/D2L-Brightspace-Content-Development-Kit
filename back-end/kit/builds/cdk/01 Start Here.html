Development Kit

Build custom interactive activities for your D2L course, with AI help, and test them against our own D2L before anything goes live.

What this is

five steps, about an hour

D2L will happily host something you build yourself: a self-marking drill, an interactive diagram, a simulation, a dashboard. What it will not do is warn you when you have built it the wrong way. Several things fail silently here, and the most expensive of them look like success right up until a student reports a problem.

This kit is the accumulated answer to that. It points you at the right approach, gives Claude the rules for building it, and checks the result against how our D2L actually behaves before any student sees it.

Four ways to build

they are not interchangeable

Two things decide which one fits. How much of the course it is allowed to see, which sets what it can do. And whether a student has to go looking for it, which sets whether they will ever see it at all. The second is the one people forget, and it causes more rebuilding than the first.

Two things cannot be combined. Step 2 will stop you before you build the wrong one, but they are worth knowing now.

  • Something that grades itself cannot know anything about your course. The trade for putting a score in the gradebook on its own is that it sits in a sealed box. It is handed the student's name and nothing else: no gradebook, no roster, and no way to tell whether the person looking is a student or you.
  • Something on the homepage cannot be graded. Homepage panels do not put scores anywhere.

Neither is much of an obstacle in practice. Build the graded activity, then build a small panel that points at it. That pairing is common, and it fixes a problem the activity has on its own: students never noticed it was there.

The process

local first, D2L last

Four things that will bite you

nothing warns you about any of these
Nothing will tell you

You will not be able to test it yourself

Open a graded activity you built and D2L shows you a preview. It looks like it works. Nothing you do in it is saved, and nothing says so. The emulator in Step 3 reproduces this on your own computer, which is where you should be catching it. For the final look in D2L, ask a TA or a colleague who is enrolled as a student — anyone with instructor permissions gets the same dead preview you do.

Nothing will tell you

Students may get one attempt when you meant three

If the activity decides too early that a student has finished, D2L closes it for good. They cannot go back and improve. You will not see a warning, and the gradebook will look perfectly normal.

Nothing will tell you

Progress can follow the computer, not the student

With some approaches, a student who starts in the lab and finishes on their own laptop begins again from nothing. Two students sharing a lab computer can end up seeing each other's work. Step 2 tells you which approaches do this.

Nothing will tell you

Students can read the answers

Anything built custom hands the student's computer everything it knows, including which answers are right. That is fine for practice. For anything that really counts, use D2L's own quiz tool, which keeps the answers on its side.

Why we are confident about this

Everything here was measured

None of the above comes from vendor documentation. Each was tested directly against our own D2L, and several findings contradict the documentation outright.

Where something has not been tested yet, the kit says so out loud instead of pretending. That habit exists because this project began with a confident, wrong belief, and nearly built everything on top of it.

Before you start

  • Claude Code, or another agent that can read a folder and run commands.
  • A course you can experiment in. Your own development shell is fine. Do not build in a course students are currently taking.
  • Nothing to request from IT. The emulator in Step 3 reproduces our D2L's behaviour on your own computer, including the parts that fail silently, so nearly all testing happens before D2L is involved at all.
  • A TA, intern, or colleague for the final look. For anything graded they need to be enrolled as a student, which is a course enrolment rather than a new account.
  • About an hour for your first activity. Much less after that.
